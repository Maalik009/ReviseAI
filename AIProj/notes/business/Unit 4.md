# Unit 4

## Operational Objectives

- Costs
- Quality
- Speed of response
- Flexibility
- Environmental
- Added value
    - Selling price - cost of inputs

## Labour Productivity

- Output during a period/Number of employees
- A business can improve labour productivity by motivating employees, providing training and investing in technology

## Unit Costs

- Total costs/Total units produced
- A business can reduce unit costs through economies of scale, lower variable costs, lean production and improve productivity

## Capacity Utilisation

- Actual Output/Maximum Output X 100
- A business should aim for 80%-90% capacity utilisation to maximise efficiency, lower unit costs, maintain flexibility and reduce strain on processes

## Lean Production

- A manufacturing approach focused on minimising waste and maximising efficiency

## Just-In-Time

- The business only receives the input when they are exactly needed in the production process
- By only receiving the inputs exactly when needed, waste is eliminated
- Allows business to hold less inventory
- In order for it to work, close relationships have to be maintained with key stakeholders

### Just-in-case

- An inventory strategy where businesses hold buffer stock to handle demand spikes or supply disruptions

## Labour Vs Capital Intensive

### Capital Intensive

- Capital intensive is a production approach that relies heavily on machinery and equipment
- Advantages:
    - Automation and machinery can produce goods faster
    - Quality is more consistent with fewer defects
    - It is easier to scale operations and meet higher demand

### Labour Intensive

- A production approach that relies more on human labour than machinery
- Advantages:
    - Less expensive to set up
    - Provides more employment (boosts the economy)
    - Allows for personalised services tailored to individual customer needs

## Quality

- Quality in operations will be determined by whether a business delivers on its targets
- High quality is achieved through training, technology, a strong culture and clear standards
- Targets:
    - Free of defects
    - Value for money
    - Fit for purpose
    - Reliability
    - Durability
    - Design
    - Delivery time
- Improving quality strengthens brand image, boosts customer loyalty and reduces costs
- TQM is a management approach where **all employees are involved in continuous quality improvement**.

## Quality Assurance Vs Quality Control

### Quality Control

- Quality control is a traditional method of **checking quality at the end of the production process** by ****using **quality inspectors** to find faults

### Quality Assurance

- A proactive process that ensures products or services meet specified standards
- Continuous improvement
    - Regularly review and improve processes
    - Quality is built into the processes

## Matching Supply To Demand

- A business aims to match supply with demand to avoid overstocking or running out of stock

### Outsourcing

- The practice of hiring external companies to perform tasks, services or produce goods

### Temporary & Part-Time Staff

- Temporary and part-time staff are brought in when demand increases

### Producing To Order

- A manufacturing approach where products are made only after a customer order is received

## Inventory Control Chart

- The **maximum inventory level** is the maximum amount of stock a business is able to hold in normal circumstances
- The **reorder level** is the level at which a business places a new order with its supplier
- The **minimum stock level** is also known as the **buffer stock** level and is the lowest level to which a business is willing to allow inventory levels to fall
- The **lead time** is the length of time from the point of inventory being ordered from the supplier to it being delivered
- Buffer stock = extra stock kept to avoid shortages.

## Managing The Supply Chain

- The network of activities, organisations, people, resources and information involved in creating a product and delivering it to the final customer
- Management:
    - Use technology and software to track inventory
    - Diversify suppliers to manage risk effectively
    - Implement inventory management (Just-in-time)
    - Implement environmental and ethical standards
    - Build strong long-term relationships (Reliable suppliers)
- **Offshoring:** Moving production to another country (often to reduce costs).
- **Reshoring:** Bringing production back to the business’s home country.
