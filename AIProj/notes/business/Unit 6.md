# Soft vs Hard HRM

- Soft HRM

- Hard HRM
    - A cost-focused approach that treats employees as resources to maximise efficiency and productivity

# Organisational Structures

- Functional
    - Groups business functions into teams, enabling expertise sharing but potentially causing coordination issues
- Product-Based
    - Divides into teams by product, allowing focus but potentially leading to internal competition and duplicated efforts if not coordinated
- Regional
    - Groups teams by different geographic regions, allowing businesses to better respond to local needs
- Matrix
    - Employees report to multiple managers, promote collaberation

# Organisational Design

- Span Of Control
    - The number of subordinates or employees a manager oversees
    - Wide span of control
        - Looking after more subordinates, giving them more autonomy and freedom, fewer levels in hierarchy
    - Narrow span of control
        - Looking after less subordinates, help the employees and skills better
    
- Delegation
    - A manager assigns work to their subordinates
    - Can be an effective training as it empowers them by providing hand-on experience

# Centralised vs Decentralised

- Centralisation
    - Where decision-making authority is concentrated at the top levels of an organisation
    - Advantages:
        - Allows for quick-decision making, beneficial in urgent situations
        - Can lead to economies of scale by centralising operations and resources
        - Reduces duplication of efforts and resources across departments
- Decentralisation
    - The distribution of decision-making authority to lower levels of the organisation
    - Advantages:
        - Empowerment and autonomy for lower managers improve motivation
        - Increases local responsiveness, allowing for better decisions due to local knowledge
        - Develops managerial talent through hands-on decision-making experience

# HR Flow

- HR Plan
    - Assesses a business’s workforce needs, identifies gaps in skills or staffing levels, aligns with corporate objectives and outlines actions to address these gaps
- Recruitment
    - Recruitment and selection is the process of attracting candidates and assessing them to determine their sustainability for a job
    - Internal recruitment
        - Filling job vacancies by hiring or promoting employees from within the organisation
        - Jobs may need to be replaced
    - External recruitment
        - Filing job vacancies by hiring candidates from outside the organisation
        - Higher talent pool
- Training
    - Inuction training
        - The initial training given to new employees to help them integrate into the business
    - On-the-job training
        - Employees develop skills and gain experience while working in their regular job role
    - Off-the-job training
        - Employees develop new skills through courses or workshops away from their usual workplace
- Redeployment
    - An employee is moved to a different role within the organisation to retain staff and avoid redundancy (an employer ends a contract because the job is no longer needed)

# Theories Of Motivation

- Taylor
    - Employees should get a fair days pay for a fair days work
    - Financial reward
    - Workers pay is determined by their performance, with higher output rewarded through piece-rate pay
- Maslow’s Hierarchy Of Needs
    - Physiological
        - Basic needs
    - Safety
        - Protection from physical and mental harm, job security
    - Social
        - Employees forming connections and collaboration
    - Esteem
        - Desire for recognition and respect, sense of accomplishment
    - Self-actualisation
        - Reaching full potential in life
- Herzberg’s Two Factor Theory
    - Linked to job enrichment because it enhances motivation through greater responsibility and opportunities for growth
    - Motivation factors - Job satisfaction
    - Examples:
        - Sense of achievement
        - Nature of job
        - Personal growth
        - Recognition
        - Opportunity for promotion
        - More responsibility
    - Hygiene factors - Job dissatisfaction
    - Examples:
        - Adequate pay
        - Working conditions
        - Supervision
        - Policies
        - Relationships
        - Job security

# Financial Methods Of Motivation

- Financial
    - Piece-rate Pay
    - Comission
    - Salary Schemes
    - Performance-related Pay
- Non-Financial
    - Empowerment
        - Employees are given full autonomy to make decisions and take control for their work
        - Enables greater skill development and personal growth for employees as they take on more responsibility
    - Team Working
        - Brings belonging, communication, shared responsibility
    - Flexible Working
        - Working from home
        - Flexible hours
        - Compressed workweek
        - Part-time roles
        - Can negatively impact productivity:
        - Home distractions
        - Work and home life
        - Lack of supervision
    - Job enrichment
        - A strategy designed to make jobs more challenging, interesting and rewarding
        - Adds depth and variety to make their roles more fulfilling and challenging
    - Job rotation
        - Involves transferring employees between roles at the same level within the hierarchy (not higher up)
        - Aims to reduce monotonous tasks wnd provide employees with more variety, helping them develop new skills
        
    

# Employer - Employee Relations

- Employees & Decision-Making
    - Factors affecting employees involvement:
        - Corporate culture(power,role,task,person)
        - Leadership style
        - Type of decisions being made
        - Skills, competencies and experience of employees
        - Legal requirements
- Trade Unions
    - Independent organisations that represents the interests of their members within a specific industry or workplace
    - Higher wages, improve working conditions, against discrimination
    - Strikes may occur if negotiations fail to reach an agreement
- Work Councils
    - Facilitates communication between employees and employers, allowing discussions on workplace issues and conditions
    - Improve communication and collaboration and operates within the business
- Value Of Good Employee - Employer Relations
    - Improves motivation, enhances communication, reduces conflicts and strengthen employer branding
