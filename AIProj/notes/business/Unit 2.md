## Management vs Leadership

- Managers
    - Responsible for planning, organizing, leading and controlling a business’s activities and resources
    - Senior Managers :
    
      - CEO’S, CFO’S, CMO’S, etc
    
      - Responsible for strategic direction
    
      - Make high level-decisions
    
      - Focus on long-term goals
    
    - Middle-Level Managers :
    
      - Head of departments, regional managers, etc
    
      - Implement strategies and policies
    
      - Allocate resources and manage budgets
    
    - Lower-Level Managers
    
      - Supervisors, team leaders, etc
    
      - Oversee day-to-day activities
    
      - Focus on short-term goals
    
      - Manage and support from front line staff
    
- Leaders
    - Someone who guides, motivates and inspires others to achieve common goals, regardless of authority

Management focuses on executing tasks and maintaining order, while leadership inspires vision and drives change for the future.

## Leadership Styles

- Autocratic
    - Makes decisions alone, exercises full control, and expects strict obedience from employees
    - Advantages :
    
      - Quicker decision-making
    
      - Leader’s strong vision provides clarity and direction to their team
    
      - Individuals have clear roles and understanding of what is expected from them
    
- Paternalistic
    - Makes decisions in the best interest of employees, offering guidance and support while expecting loyalty and trust
    - Advantages :
    
      - Improves decision-making
    
      - Improved employee morale
    
      - Enhanced guidance and support
    
- Democratic
    - Encourages participation, values team input and involves employees in decision-making
    - Advantages :
    
      - Team members are engaged and motivated
    
      - Encourages diverse ideas and creativity
    
      - Team members take ownership of decisions
    
- Laissez-Faire
    - Provides minimal supervision, giving employees freedom to make decisions and manage their work
    - Advantages :
    
      - Team members have high autonomy which can increase job satisfaction
    
      - Fosters creativity and innovation
    
      - Allows experts to make decisions in their specialised areas

## Scientific vs Intuition Decision-Making

- Scientific Decision-Making
    - An approach that uses data, analysis and evidence to make informed business decisions
- Intuition Decision-Making
    - Relies on gut feelings, instincts and personal experience rather than data analysis

## Risk, Uncertainty And Opportunity Cost

- Risk
    - The potential for negative outcomes where the probabilities of different outcomes are known
- Uncertainty
    - The situations where the outcomes of decisions are unknown and the probabilities of these outcomes cannot be accurately predicted or quantified
    - Examples :
    
     - Economic conditions
    
     - Competitor actions
    
     - Global events
    
- Opportunity Cost
    - The value of the next best alternative foregone when a choice is made

## Influences On Decision-Making

- Mission & Objectives
- Ethics
    - Principles that govern which behaviors are morally acceptable to society, individuals or groups
    - Will typically involve a trade-off with profit
- External Environment
    - If the external environment is going through rapid change, this makes decisions-making difficult due to high amounts of risk and uncertainty
- Resource Constraints
