# SWOT Analysis

![image.png](/static/business/image%2032.png)

- Strengths are internal advantages that give a business a competitive advantage, such as strong branding or efficient processes
- Weaknesses are internal limitations that may hinder business success, like poor financial performance or outdated technology
- Opportunities are external factors that a business can exploit for growth, such as emerging markets or changing trends
- Threats are external challenges that could harm a business, like new competitors or changing consumer trends

# Balance Sheets

- A balance sheet is a financial statement that shows a business’s assets, liabilities and equity
- Assets = Liabilities + Equity
- Net assets = Total assets  - Total liabilities
- Current - Within 12 months
- Non-current - After 12 months

## Current Assets

- Expected to be converted into cash or used up within one year

## Non-Current Assets

- Not expected to be converted into cash or used up within one year, but are kept to help the business generate economic benefits

## Current Liabilities

- Debt that is due to be paid within one year

## Non-Current Liabilities

- Long-term debt that is due to be paid after one year

## Equity

- The owner’s claim on the business assets after all liabilities are paid

# Income Statements

![image.png](/static/business/image%2033.png)

- Revenue
    - The total amount of money earned by a company from its normal business operations, typically from the sale of goods and services to customers
    - Revenue = Price x Quantity sold
- Cost of sales
    - The direct costs incurred in producing or purchasing the goods or services that a business sells, such as raw materials and labour
- Operating Expenses
    - The costs associated with running a company’s day-to-day operations, but they do not include the direct costs of producing goods or services
    - Salaries & wages, marketing, rent, R&D, utilities
- Finance costs
    - The expenses incurred by a business for borrowing money, such as interest paid on loans, overdrafts, or bonds
- Taxation
    - The amount of tax a business owes on its profits, including corporation tax or income tax
- Calculations
    - Gross profit = Revenue - Cost of sales
    - Operating profit = Gross profit - Operating expenses
    - Net profit (Before tax) = Operating profit - Finance costs
    - Net profit (After Tax) = Net profit (Before tax) - Taxation

# Gearing

- Gearing aims to determine the optimal capital structure for a business, balancing debt and equity
- Capital employed is the total amount of capital a business uses to operate
- Capital employed refers to the total long-term investments made in a business to support its operations and growth
- Capital employed = Non-Current liabilites + Equitiy

![image.png](/static/business/image%2034.png)

- Low gearing: Below 25%
    - A low gearing ratio indicates that a business relies more on equity that long-term debt for is long-term financing
    - Low gearing may indicate limited growth potential, as the business is avoiding risks and not raising sufficient capital through debt
- High gearing: Above 50%
    - A high gearing ratio indicates that a business relies heavily on long-term debt compared to equity for its long-term financing
    - High gearing increases interest costs, financial risk during economic downturns, the likelihood of insolvency and pressures on cash flow
- Optimal gearing: Between 25% and 50%

# ROCE

- ROCE is a financial performance ratio that measures profitability and efficiency in using capital

![image.png](/static/business/image%2036.png)

- Ratio analysis is purely quantitative, ignoring qualitative factors and it relies on accurate financial statements

# Efficiency Ratios

- Payable days
    - Trade payables (creditors) days measure the average time a business takes to pay its suppliers or other creditors
    - A business may aim for longer payable days to improve cash flow, but this could strain relationships with suppliers
    
    ![image.png](/static/business/image%2038.png)
    
- Receivable days
    - Receivables (debtor) days measure the average time it takes for a business to collect payment from customers who owe money
    - A business may want lower receivables days, but this could discourage customers who prefer a longer credit period
    - A business would aim to have lower receivables days than payables days to improve its liquidity position
    
    ![image.png](/static/business/image%2039.png)
    
- Inventory Turnover
    - Inventory turnover measures how many times a business sells and replaces its inventory within a given time period (typically a year)
    - Inventory turnover is industry-specific
    - To improve inventory turnover, a business can hold less stock or increase sales volume

# Elkington’s Triple Bottom Line

- The triple bottom line (TBL) considers three key factors:
    - People (social impact)
    - Planet (environmental impact)
    - Profit
- The people element examines the business’s impact on stakeholders, ensuring fair pay, proper treatment and consideration in decision-making
- The planet element focuses on minimising environmental damage by reducing carbon emissions, using sustainable resources and adapting eco-friendly practices
- The profit element ensures financial focus while balancing social and environmental concerns (reduces short-term profits)
