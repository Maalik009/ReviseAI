## Financial Objectives

- Revenue
    - Revenue growth or sales maximisation
- Cost and profit
    - Cost minimsation focuses on reducing expenses to the lowest possible level without compromising the quality of products
    - Profit maximisation focuses on achieving the highest possible level of profit within a specific time frame
- Cash flow objectives
    - Essential for the success and survival of a business
    - Net cash flow = Cash inflows - cash outflows
- Return on investment
    - Helps business measure profitability, allocate resources effectively and evaluate the success of projects
    - ROI = Profit from investment/cost of investment X 100
    
    ## Cash flow Vs Profit
    
- Cash flow is the money flowing in and out of the business
- Profit money made when total revenue takeaway total costs

## Profitability

- A measure of how efficiently a business generates profit from its revenue or operations
- Main method of calculating profitability is by calculating profit margins
    - Profit/revenue X 100
- It is possible for a business to generate more profit while having a lower profit margin

## Budgeting

- Financial plan that outlines expected income and expenditure over a specific period
- A budget will typically cover income, expenditure and profit
- Variance = Actual value - budgeted value
    - The results are either favourable or adverse
    - Favourable: Revenue is higher than expected, cost is lower than expected
    - Adverse: Revenue is lower than expected, cost is higher than expected
- Advantages
    - Helps monitor and control income and expenses effectively
    - Provides clear financial targets, which can motivate employees
    - Assists the business in allocating resources more efficiently

## Break-Even

- Shows the number of units a business needs to sell for its total revenue to equal its total costs
- For break-even to work, assume constant variable costs, a fixed selling price, all units sold and stable fixed costs
- Total contribution = Revenue - total variable costs
- Contribution per unit = Price - variable cost per unit
- Break-even = Fixed costs/selling price - variable costs (contribution per unit)

## Cash Flow Forecasts

- The movement of money into and out of a business over a specific period of time
- Net cash flow = Cash inflows - cash outflows
- Closing balance = Net cash flow + opening balance

## Payables Vs Receivables

- A trade receivable is any money that is owed to the business by their debtors
- A trade payable is when a business has purchased goods or services from creditors but has not yet paid them

## Internal Finance

- Retained profits
    - If the business still has profit left over after deciding how much to pay shareholders, this is reinvested as retained profit
    - Cost-effective as it doesn't need to be repaid, making it ideal when a business is highly profitable
    - Depends on businesses profit level and may result in lower dividends for shareholders
- Sale of assets
    - A source of finance where a business raises funds by selling unused or unnecessary resources
    - Reduces business costs since the asset is no longer in use
    - The business may need the asset in the future for operations, potentially reducing its capacity

## External Finance

- Debt factoring
    - Provides an instant cash inflow to solve short-term cash flow problems and reduces time spent chasing invoices
    - A portion of revenue is given away, reducing profit margins and the customer deals with the factoring company
- Bank overdraft
    - A short-term facility allowing a business to withdraw more than its account balance, up to a set limit
    - High interest rates, increases business costs
- Share capital
    - Can raise large amounts of finance especially for PLCs and the finance does not need to be repaid
    - It dilutes ownership of the business and the business pays dividends across a large number of shareholders
- Loans
    - A borrowed sum that businesses repay over time, usually with interest under agreed terms
    - Smaller businesses are often perceived as higher risk, leading to higher interest rates on loans
    - Loans can provide substantial finance and support cash flow, with repayments spread over time
    - Interest payments increase costs, and businesses must make repayments regardless of performance
- Crowdfunding
    - A way to raise funds by gathering small contributions from many people to finance a business, project or idea
    - No guarantee of receiving the full amount, as it is competitive and ideas become public
- Venture capital
    - A private equity funding for startups or small businesses with high growth potential in exchange for ownership
    - Offers expert guidance, mentorship, networking and funding without the need for repayment
    - Is usually shared, impacting decision-making as venture capitalists seek high returns
